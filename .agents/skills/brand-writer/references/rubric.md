# Simtropolis brand voice rubric

Score each criterion from 1 to 5. Copy passes only when every criterion scores at least 4.

## 1. Technical grounding

Does the copy name a concrete mechanism or observable product behavior?

- **5:** Every material product statement identifies behavior, configuration, or evidence a developer can inspect.
- **4:** Claims are concrete and technically meaningful, with minor compression for the format.
- **3:** Specific details sit beside vague product language.
- **2:** Technical terms decorate mostly abstract claims.
- **1:** The copy contains no technical substance.

Good: “Copper includes Rust and `rust-analyzer` defaults, Cargo actions, test exploration, a terminal, Git, and debugging.”

Weak: “Copper provides a powerful Rust workflow.”

## 2. Natural syntax

Does the writing sound natural when read aloud?

- **5:** Sentence lengths and structures vary without calling attention to themselves.
- **4:** Mostly natural, with a small amount of visible structure.
- **3:** Formulaic transitions, fragments, or tidy parallelism are noticeable.
- **2:** Repeated triples, em-dash chains, or slogan rhythms dominate.
- **1:** The copy reads like a generated template.

## 3. Quiet confidence

Does the copy state what is true without hype or emotional pressure?

- **5:** Facts and boundaries carry the message.
- **4:** Direct and restrained, with only minor flourish.
- **3:** Occasional superlatives or promotional framing appear.
- **2:** The copy repeatedly tells the reader what to feel.
- **1:** Hype replaces substance.

## 4. Developer respect

Does the copy treat the reader as a technical peer and preserve their authority?

- **5:** It assumes competence, names tradeoffs, and explains what the developer reviews or decides.
- **4:** Respectful and clear, with appropriate technical depth.
- **3:** Some simplification or vague “AI handles it” language.
- **2:** Patronizing instructions or inflated claims of agent autonomy.
- **1:** The developer is treated as a passive buyer or obstacle.

## 5. Information priority

Is the most useful fact first for the surface?

- **5:** The product, change, status, or required action leads immediately.
- **4:** The lede is clear after a brief, useful setup.
- **3:** The point is recoverable but buried.
- **2:** Philosophy or company narrative delays the answer.
- **1:** The key information is absent.

## 6. Specificity and status

Are claims concrete, and can the reader tell what is available?

- **5:** Every capability is specific and clearly labeled available, opt-in, experimental, recorded, or planned where relevant.
- **4:** Status and behavior are clear with only immaterial omissions.
- **3:** Some capabilities or access conditions are ambiguous.
- **2:** Roadmap and shipped behavior blur together.
- **1:** The copy makes broad claims without boundaries.

## 7. Voice and product consistency

Does the tone stay coherent, and are Simtropolis, Made, Copper, and upstream Zed kept distinct?

- **5:** One calm voice and a correct product model throughout.
- **4:** Minor tonal variation appropriate to the surface; identities remain clear.
- **3:** Noticeable shifts or loose use of product names.
- **2:** The copy repeatedly conflates company, platform, product, or upstream.
- **1:** Identity and voice are inconsistent.

## 8. Earned claims

Can every factual assertion be traced to an authoritative source?

- **5:** All material claims, benefits, release facts, and availability statements are verified; mechanisms support benefits.
- **4:** Claims are verified and supportable, with minor non-factual connective language.
- **3:** One or more assertions lack a source or overstate the evidence.
- **2:** Several claims depend on assumption, roadmap material, or borrowed upstream messaging.
- **1:** The message relies on invented proof or unsupported superlatives.

## Scorecard

```text
| Criterion                     | Score | Notes |
|-------------------------------|------:|-------|
| Technical grounding           |   /5  |       |
| Natural syntax                |   /5  |       |
| Quiet confidence              |   /5  |       |
| Developer respect             |   /5  |       |
| Information priority          |   /5  |       |
| Specificity and status        |   /5  |       |
| Voice and product consistency |   /5  |       |
| Earned claims                 |   /5  |       |
| Total                         |  /40  |       |
```

Any score below 4 requires revision. Re-check facts and status labels after the humanizer pass.
