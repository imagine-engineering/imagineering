# Rejected language and patterns

These rules apply to public Simtropolis, Made, and Copper copy unless a term appears in an attributed quotation or must be discussed during review.

## Unsupported product language

Reject:

- autonomous or fully autonomous
- revolutionary, game-changing, cutting-edge, next-generation, groundbreaking
- seamless, frictionless, effortless
- powerful, robust, intuitive, innovative, elegant without a concrete explanation
- supercharge, turbocharge, unlock, empower, leverage
- best-in-class, world-class, state-of-the-art, industry-leading
- smarter, faster, better, more productive without a verified comparison or measure
- “agents replace developers” or any equivalent claim

Do not invent or import:

- performance figures or benchmarks;
- customer names, logos, testimonials, or adoption claims;
- integrations, model providers, certifications, security guarantees, retention policies, or pricing;
- supported platforms, architectures, signing, auto-update behavior, or release access;
- Zed claims about performance, collaboration, history, or team identity.

## Ambiguous category words

Review every use of **agentic**, **environment**, **platform**, **workflow**, **control**, and **open**. Keep the word only when the surrounding sentence explains what it means.

- “agentic” should identify the agent action, tools, evidence, or review boundary;
- “environment” should identify the integrated development functions;
- “platform” should identify what products or capabilities are composed from it;
- “workflow” should name the actual sequence or tool interaction;
- “control” should name the decision or permission the developer retains;
- “open” should state whether it refers to source licensing, repository access, extension points, contribution, or inspectability.

## Status failures

Reject copy that:

- presents an opt-in feature as part of standard Copper;
- presents an experimental crate, flag, test, design note, or prototype as a shipped product capability;
- uses present tense for Groovy, Forge, or another planned product without fresh evidence;
- calls recorded output live;
- implies a browser demo runs a compiler, backend, remote service, multiplayer session, or agent when it does not;
- says an agent executed or changed something when it only proposed an action;
- calls the Made repository public without checking its current visibility.

## Formulaic structures

Rewrite:

- “It’s not X, it’s Y” and “not only X, but Y” constructions;
- rhetorical questions used as headings or openers;
- repeated slogan fragments and one-word sentence chains;
- mirrored clauses and conspicuous groups of three;
- em-dash chains or more than one em dash in a paragraph;
- a colon followed by a promotional list in ordinary prose;
- repeated definitions of the company, platform, or product on the same page.

## Empty enthusiasm and filler

Remove:

- “We’re excited/thrilled to announce”;
- “We can’t wait for you to try”;
- “You’ll love,” “Get ready,” “Say goodbye,” “Say hello,” “The wait is over”;
- “In today’s fast-paced world,” “As developers, we know,” “Let’s face it”;
- “It is important to note,” “At the end of the day,” “When it comes to”;
- standalone “Introducing…”;
- promotional exclamation points, dramatic ellipses, emoji, and all-caps emphasis.

## Generic benefits

Replace “improved workflow,” “enhanced productivity,” “better experience,” “streamlined process,” and similar phrases with the product behavior that causes the benefit. If no mechanism or evidence supports the benefit, remove it.

## Final scan

Search for superlatives, absolutes, intensifiers, vague quantifiers, synonym cycling, and claims whose source is “common knowledge.” A factual claim without an authoritative source fails even when it sounds plausible.
